## Connect the technical requirement to the purchase

Oil and gas procurement connects an equipment or service requirement to a clearly defined commercial commitment. The work includes preparing an enquiry, identifying appropriate suppliers, comparing offers and coordinating the information needed for delivery. This category reflects the sourcing and procurement subjects already described on the Oillinko website. It is intended for buyers assembling a first request, engineers supporting technical evaluation and traders coordinating several product families. The objective is a purchase description that both sides understand, with assumptions and unresolved questions recorded rather than hidden behind a short product name.

A procurement process can start from an approved datasheet, an equipment list, a replacement part number or an initial project concept. Each starting point has different information gaps. A complete manufacturer code may still need confirmation of revision or availability, while a new process duty may need technical selection before a firm price is meaningful. Identify the maturity of the requirement and the purpose of the enquiry. A budget estimate, a technically evaluated offer and an order-ready quotation should not be treated as interchangeable stages of the same commercial decision.

## Structure the request around the buying question

The [supplier selection and RFQ category](category:supplier-selection-rfq) explains how to identify supplier fit and prepare comparable requests. Start with the item or service boundary, required quantity, relevant specification and any constraints on alternatives. An enquiry for an exact spare should distinguish mandatory identity from a preference for an equivalent. For a mixed equipment list, separate the product families and identify which documents apply to each line. This makes it easier for a supplier to answer the relevant questions without assuming responsibility for unrelated parts of the project.

The article on [writing a bill of quantities](post:how-to-write-a-bill-of-quantities) provides a practical introduction to that structure. A useful line item identifies the product, quantity, unit and technical basis, while supporting drawings and datasheets carry the details that do not fit neatly in a schedule. Maintain revision control across these documents. When a clarification changes a requirement, make sure the updated information reaches every supplier participating in the comparison. Otherwise the apparent difference in price may simply reflect different versions of the requested scope.

## Evaluate suppliers against the actual scope

Supplier suitability depends on the required product, configuration, project conditions and delivery expectations. A company that offers a broad catalogue may not manufacture the item, hold it in stock or have authority to propose every listed brand. Ask for the relevant evidence and distinguish the manufacturer's role from that of a distributor or sourcing intermediary. The article comparing [trading companies and direct manufacturer purchasing](post:equipment-trading-companies-vs-buying-direct) explains why either route can be useful depending on the requirement. The decision should follow the scope rather than an assumption that one route always provides the better outcome.

Oillinko's [global company directory](/oil-and-gas/companies) is an independent reference organized by company role and specialty. A directory entry does not establish a supplier agreement, current stock or an authorized commercial relationship with Oillinko. Use it to understand the industry and identify relevant areas for enquiry, then verify the specific supplier and offered product. This is particularly important when a quotation names a manufacturer but the commercial counterparty is another business. The order should make clear who supplies the goods, who supports the warranty and where the supporting product evidence comes from.

## Compare complete offers and recorded deviations

Separate the technical comparison from the commercial comparison while keeping them connected. First establish whether the offered product or service meets the agreed requirement and identify exceptions requiring approval. Then compare price, delivery, documentation, packing, warranty and any excluded scope. A lower unit price may exclude testing or accessories included elsewhere. Request a written deviations schedule rather than trying to infer differences from scattered comments. The final purchase description should incorporate the accepted technical clarification, not simply refer to the earliest enquiry number.

Quality evidence is part of that comparison, but its detail belongs in a clear acceptance specification. Use the [inspection and material quality guide](category:inspection-material-quality) to define inspection scope, material records and traceability. Requiring a certificate without naming its purpose can add paperwork without answering the project's acceptance question. Conversely, requesting essential evidence after manufacture may create delays or reveal that the necessary activity was never planned. Agree the required deliverables before placement and identify review or witness points that could affect the production schedule.

## Plan delivery as part of the purchase

Delivery terms should identify the agreed place and the allocation of responsibilities, not merely a three-letter abbreviation. The [ICC Incoterms resource](https://iccwbo.org/business-solutions/incoterms-rules/incoterms-2020/) explains the rules used to allocate relevant costs, risks and obligations in a sale of goods. Payment terms, product acceptance and other contractual matters still need their own agreement. The [logistics and delivery guide](category:logistics-delivery) develops the practical questions about equipment readiness, packing, multi-vendor consolidation and handover records that should be addressed before goods are released.

Ask what triggers the quoted lead time and distinguish manufacturing completion from arrival at the project destination. Drawing approval, advance payment, inspection release and shipping arrangements can each affect the schedule. Record any dependencies and the process for reporting changes. For long or complex orders, request progress information tied to meaningful milestones rather than a repeated promise that the goods are on schedule. The article on [consolidated shipments](post:consolidated-shipments-and-multi-vendor-orders) explains why coordinating several ready dates can be as important as selecting the transport mode.

## Submit a requirement with clear next steps

The [Oillinko procurement services page](/services) describes the route for discussing sourcing, RFQ coordination and related purchase requirements. Send the current specification, quantities, destination and required date, and mark any questions that still need resolution. Enquiries are received by Oillinko for review and coordination; an enquiry does not establish stock availability or create a supplier commitment. A useful next step is the clarification or quotation needed to advance the specific requirement. Keeping that step explicit helps the buyer, technical reviewer and commercial counterpart work from the same expectations.
