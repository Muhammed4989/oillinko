## Match the pumping principle to the duty

Pumps and rotating equipment are bought to meet a process duty, not simply to match a pipe diameter or motor rating. This category covers centrifugal, positive displacement and metering pumps, together with mechanical seals, couplings and the information used to assess performance and acceptance. These subjects reflect the existing pump catalogue and published Oillinko articles. Compressors and turbines also appear in the wider equipment catalogue, but gas compression and turbine selection require their own manufacturer assessments; a liquid pump selection method should not be applied to them without checking its relevance.

Centrifugal pumps are often considered for continuous liquid transfer, while positive displacement designs may be considered where viscosity, pressure or controlled delivery changes the selection problem. Neither description establishes suitability on its own. A chemical dosing duty, for example, requires attention to achievable flow control and liquid end compatibility, whereas an intake pump requires attention to the source and suction arrangement. The comparison of [reciprocating and rotary positive displacement pumps](post:api-674-api-676-positive-displacement-pumps-explained) helps buyers distinguish these families before requesting a detailed selection from a manufacturer.

## Give the supplier a duty envelope

Specify required flow, differential head or pressures, fluid temperature and the expected range of operation. Identify density, viscosity, entrained gas and solids where relevant, with the basis and units used for each value. A supplier may need additional fluid information to evaluate materials, hydraulic behavior or sealing. Explain whether operation is continuous, intermittent, variable or shared between parallel machines. A selection that addresses the rated point alone may not describe the lowest or highest duty the system will experience, so request the proposed operating envelope and any limitations explicitly.

A pump curve is a way to examine the offered machine, rather than a substitute for the system information. [KSB's explanation of characteristic curves](https://www.ksb.com/en-global/centrifugal-pump-lexicon/article/characteristic-curve-1117926) identifies flow, head, power, efficiency and required NPSH as related plotted quantities. The Oillinko guide to [reading a pump curve](post:reading-a-pump-curve-explained) explains the buying questions these curves raise. Check the offered speed, impeller configuration, test fluid and relevant corrections. The system designer must establish the available suction conditions and the required margin for the actual installation.

## Specify the complete rotating assembly

A pump package can include the pump, driver, coupling, baseplate, guards and auxiliary systems, but suppliers do not all quote the same boundary. State whether the motor and electrical controls are included, which power supply is available, and who is responsible for mounting and alignment arrangements. Existing foundation drawings and maintenance access can constrain a replacement. The article on [couplings and baseplate design](post:api-671-couplings-and-baseplate-design) is useful when a pump fits hydraulically but the complete rotating assembly still needs a mechanical and installation review.

Sealing arrangements deserve a separate discussion. The process fluid, emissions requirements, seal chamber conditions and available utilities affect the proposed seal and its support system. Flush, buffer and barrier arrangements serve different purposes and should not be selected solely by the familiarity of a plan number. Read the [mechanical seal piping plan guide](post:api-682-mechanical-seal-piping-plans-explained) alongside the supplier's seal drawing and auxiliary system description. Confirm the supply boundary for reservoirs, coolers, instruments and connecting pipework, and ask who approves the support fluid and operating requirements for the project.

## Treat standards as an agreed specification

The [API 610 pump type guide](post:api-610-pump-types-and-classes-explained) introduces the equipment configurations commonly discussed in petroleum projects. A type designation helps describe construction, but it does not establish every material, performance or accessory requirement. The RFQ should identify the standard edition and purchaser supplements that apply. If the supplier proposes an alternative configuration, ask for a written explanation of fit, duty, maintenance implications and deviations. The owner's technical acceptance is still needed, even where two offers use similar product descriptions or claim the same general standard.

For controlled dosing, the [metering pump article](post:api-675-metering-pumps-controlled-volume-dosing-explained) adds questions about adjustment range, accuracy basis, repeatability and the proposed liquid end. Request the actual performance guarantee and its test conditions rather than assuming that every model has the same behavior throughout its adjustment range. Include chemical concentration, suction arrangement, control signal and expected backpressure in the enquiry. Relief and protective arrangements need to follow the approved system design and manufacturer instructions; a purchasing guide cannot set safe operating values for an unidentified pump.

## Agree evidence and maintainability before ordering

Factory testing should demonstrate the agreed requirements using an approved test procedure and defined acceptance criteria. Decide which records are required, which activities need witnessing and how a deviation will be resolved before release. The [pump acceptance testing guide](post:pump-factory-acceptance-testing-and-commissioning-checklist) explains how to frame those contractual questions. Factory acceptance does not establish that the installed piping, utilities and controls are ready for operation. Site checks and commissioning remain a separate scope under the responsible project team and the equipment manufacturer's instructions.

For a replacement or spare, provide the complete equipment identification and distinguish an individual part from a complete assembly. Ask about drawings, recommended spares, preservation, storage conditions and support documentation when comparing total cost. Submit the duty through the [pump requirements catalogue](/oil-and-gas/equipment/pumps), or define a separate [inspection and repair enquiry](/oil-and-gas/services/inspection-engineering) when the immediate need is condition assessment. This keeps a potential repair decision separate from the commercial assumption that a new pump is already required.
