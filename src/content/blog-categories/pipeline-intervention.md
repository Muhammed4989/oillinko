## Understand the purpose of an intervention enquiry

Pipeline intervention enquiries describe work on an existing line where operating constraints, asset condition and pressure containment are central to the project. This category focuses on hot tapping and line stopping, reflecting the existing Oillinko article and catalogue subjects. Hot tapping creates a connection to an existing pressure boundary using a specialist engineered arrangement. Line stopping provides a means of isolating a section within a defined intervention scheme. Neither technique should be assumed feasible merely because suitable-looking equipment can be purchased or a similar project has been completed elsewhere.

The [hot tapping and line stopping article](post:hot-tapping-and-line-stopping) explains the terminology and the information buyers need before requesting proposals. Its role is educational and commercial scoping, not field execution. The pipeline operator and competent specialists must assess the actual asset, approve the engineering basis and determine the procedures that govern the work. Some conditions may require a different method or a shutdown. The commercial enquiry should leave room for that assessment rather than guaranteeing continued operation before feasibility and the required safeguards have been established.

## Collect line data and condition information

Provide the line identification, drawings, outside diameter, wall information and material specification available from the operator's records. Include the fluid, pressure and temperature conditions relevant to the proposed work, identifying which values are confirmed. Existing corrosion, repairs, lining, coating or other features may affect the assessment. If inspection data is incomplete, identify the additional investigation that may be needed. Do not substitute a nominal pipe schedule for verified condition information where the intervention design depends on the actual pipe at the proposed location.

The physical arrangement matters as much as the headline line size. Branch location, access, surrounding supports, nearby equipment and installation orientation can constrain the intervention. Provide site photographs and drawings as supporting information, and state whether excavation, insulation removal or structural access is expected. The provider should identify what site verification is required before finalizing the proposal. A preliminary quotation can be useful for planning, but its assumptions should remain visible so it is not treated as a fully approved method for an unverified worksite.

## Define the isolation objective and operating constraints

Explain why the intervention is being considered and what part of the system needs to be modified or isolated. A new branch connection, a planned equipment replacement and a local repair can require different arrangements and acceptance evidence. State the intended work window and any constraints on flow, shutdown duration or access. These are requirements for assessment, not proof that a particular technique can meet them. The specialist proposal should identify the boundaries of isolation, interfaces with the surrounding system and the operator responsibilities necessary to evaluate the scheme.

[HSE's safe isolation guidance](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/books/hsg253.htm) discusses the planning and selection of isolation arrangements for plant and equipment, including pipeline considerations. It provides context for why isolation needs a documented basis rather than reliance on a generic equipment label. The guidance is from the United Kingdom; applicable local requirements, the operator's engineering standards and approved site procedures remain essential. This category deliberately avoids operational settings, welding instructions and pressure limits because those cannot be established safely for an unidentified line through a general purchasing article.

## Match equipment to the engineered arrangement

The supply package can include fittings, valves, tapping equipment, stopping equipment and associated accessories, but the exact configuration depends on the approved design. Ask the provider to identify compatibility between components and the pressure boundary of the assembled system. Nominal size or flange class alone does not demonstrate that equipment from different systems can be used together. Proprietary interfaces may require specific parts and procedures. If equipment is purchased separately from the service, identify who takes responsibility for confirming the complete arrangement and its suitability for the project.

Distinguish permanent components left on the pipeline from temporary equipment used during the intervention. The documentation, inspection and handover requirements may differ between them. Permanent fittings need the material, design and traceability evidence specified by the project, while temporary equipment may need relevant identification, inspection history and suitability records. The [materials documentation guide](category:materials-documentation) explains how to connect records to delivered components. The [pipeline intervention equipment catalogue](/oil-and-gas/equipment/pipeline-intervention) provides a route for submitting the identified product requirement without implying approval of the field method.

## Specify service responsibilities and acceptance

A service offer should state whether engineering, mobilization, specialist personnel, equipment, consumables, inspection and final reporting are included. Identify who provides access, lifting support, utilities and site preparation. The operator should define its permit, isolation and emergency arrangements through the responsible project team. Ask the provider to explain qualification requirements and any subcontracted activities relevant to the scope. These are matters to agree before execution; a broad statement that a contractor follows an industry standard does not replace project-specific evidence and approval.

Completion criteria should describe the agreed inspections, records and handover package, including any limitations or unresolved findings. Changes in site condition or scope should have an agreed review process and commercial basis. Use the [pipeline and process services catalogue](/oil-and-gas/services/pipeline-process) when the enquiry concerns a complete specialist service rather than individual equipment items. Read the [oil and gas services guide](category:oil-and-gas-services) for wider scoping principles. Oillinko receives the requirement for review and provider coordination, while feasibility and execution remain subject to the appropriate specialist and operator approvals.
