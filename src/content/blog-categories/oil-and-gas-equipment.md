## Start with the process requirement

Oil and gas equipment selection begins with the task a system must perform. A replacement pump, a new vessel and an instrument spare each require a different technical conversation, even when they belong to the same project. This category organizes those conversations around equipment function. It covers rotating machinery, pressure boundary components, process and utility packages, instrumentation and well equipment already represented in the Oillinko catalogue. The aim is to help buyers prepare usable requirements and recognize missing information before a quotation becomes the basis of a purchase order.

The first distinction is between replacing an identified item and designing a new equipment duty. For a replacement, the installed manufacturer's name, complete model, serial number and drawing revision can be more useful than a broad description. For a new duty, the process conditions and system interfaces determine what a manufacturer can propose. A photograph supports identification but rarely proves material, pressure rating or internal construction. Keep uncertain details visible as questions, because turning an assumption into a firm specification can exclude the appropriate supplier or result in an unsuitable offer.

## Compare equipment as part of a system

Rotating equipment moves or pressurizes fluids, while valves and piping control and contain them. Vessels and heat exchangers provide separation, storage or heat transfer, and instruments measure the conditions that operators need to understand. These functions intersect, but their selection methods differ. Use the [pumps and rotating equipment guide](category:pumps-rotating-equipment) for machine duty and performance questions. Use [valves, piping and sealing](category:piping-flanges-fittings) when the main concern is a connection, isolation function or pressure boundary. This division keeps the buying discussion focused without treating the surrounding system as irrelevant.

An equipment package also has boundaries that may not appear in its headline description. A pump quotation might include a driver but exclude its starter; an actuated valve may exclude the local control panel; a vessel offer may stop at the nozzle flanges. Establish which party supplies supports, cables, tubing, insulation, utility connections and installation work. Ask for an explicit scope drawing when several suppliers meet at the same interface. A lower equipment price can represent a smaller scope, so comparisons need to consider the completed installation rather than the main component alone.

## Record normal and exceptional conditions

Process information should describe the conditions equipment will actually encounter. Fluid identity, composition, temperature, pressure and required capacity are useful starting points, but a single normal operating point may leave important questions unanswered. Intermittent service, startup conditions, cleaning fluids, solids or changes in feed composition can affect selection. Distinguish design conditions from routine operation and identify which values are confirmed. Suppliers should explain the assumptions behind their selections and the conditions under which any quoted performance is applicable, rather than presenting a catalogue maximum as a project guarantee.

Materials require similar care. Stainless steel is a family of materials, not a complete compatibility specification. The body, trim, elastomers, seal faces and fasteners can see different exposures and may need separate identification. A material that suits one fluid concentration or temperature may require a different assessment elsewhere. For an existing installation, provide the approved material specification and known degradation history when available. The [materials and documentation guide](category:materials-documentation) explains how to connect these requirements to the records that should accompany the actual supplied items.

## Resolve interfaces before comparing price

Mechanical fit includes more than nominal pipe size. Facing, connection standard, overall dimensions, mounting arrangements and access for maintenance all matter. Electrical and instrument interfaces add supply voltage, frequency, signal type, communications and installation environment. Where a project specifies a hazardous area classification or another approval, send the exact requirement and ask the supplier to identify the relevant product evidence. An approval attached to a different model or configuration is not automatically applicable. The [process, utilities and instrumentation guide](category:pressure-vessels-tanks) explores these package and measurement interfaces in more detail.

Project standards should be listed with the applicable edition and any purchaser amendments. Different documents may govern equipment design, connections, testing and material records. Do not infer that meeting one named standard proves compliance with every project requirement. Ask suppliers to return a compliance statement and a separate deviations schedule that your responsible engineer can evaluate. For example, a performance test and a material certificate answer different questions. Neither replaces the other, and both need acceptance criteria appropriate to the equipment and contract.

## Build a useful enquiry and reading path

A useful enquiry combines the technical requirement with quantity, required delivery date, destination and documentation scope. State whether alternatives are welcome, whether the item must match an installed unit, and which dimensions or interfaces cannot change. For well-related purchases, begin with the [wellhead and well equipment guide](category:wellhead-production-equipment), where identification and assembly boundaries are especially important. For all equipment families, retain the supplier's clarification responses alongside the quotation so that the final purchase description reflects what was agreed rather than an earlier, incomplete version of the enquiry.

The [equipment catalogue](/oil-and-gas/equipment) provides the practical route from these explanations to a requirement submitted to Oillinko. Catalogue coverage describes the subjects on which an enquiry can be made; availability, manufacturer suitability and supply scope must still be confirmed for the individual order. Read the article on [preparing a bill of quantities](post:how-to-write-a-bill-of-quantities) before assembling a mixed equipment list. It helps separate line items, units and supporting documents so that technical review and commercial comparison can proceed from the same information.
