# Offshore and water sector guides

Reviewed 2 October 2026. Two original procurement guides expand the existing `/industries/offshore` and `/industries/water` pages without creating taxonomy or changing canonical routes. Paragraph-only counts are 760 and 770 words respectively. They explain sector-wide work boundaries rather than repeat a particular equipment article.

The guides use native disclosure controls in the main content column, retain one H1, and link to existing topic pages through `typeUrl`. Original illustrative assets already used by the site provide descriptive English alt and matching hover text through `SiteImage`. Supplier identities remain confined to the company directory. No stock, price, project approval or treatment guarantee is implied.

## Primary editorial basis

- [IMO ballast water management technologies](https://www.imo.org/en/ourwork/environment/pages/bwmtechnologies.aspx): the ballast-water example distinguishes Administration type approval for the actual system from general marine equipment supply. It does not apply that approval framework to every offshore product.
- [IMO ballast water FAQ](https://www.imo.org/en/ourwork/environment/pages/bwmfaq.aspx): installation and survey responsibilities need identification separately from product approval.
- [IFC offshore development EHS guidance](https://www.ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doc/2010/2015-offshore-oil-gas-development-ehs-guidelines-en.pdf): produced-water constituents and treatment boundaries. No numerical discharge criteria are reproduced or presented as universal requirements.
- [IFC general EHS guidelines](https://www.ifc.org/en/insights-reports/general-environmental-health-and-safety-guidelines): project-specific applicability and assessment. Guidance is context, not a replacement for permits or local law.
- [ILAC guidance series](https://ilac.org/publications-and-resources/ilac-guidance-series/): measurement uncertainty and conformity decision rules, consistent with the existing calibration guide.

Remaining copy is original enquiry-planning guidance. Project engineering determines suitability, operating limits and acceptance. New text asks for the approved basis; it does not supply operating instructions.
